# 🏆 Hackathon Judge Q&A Cheat Sheet
## "Quantum Heist: Break the Entanglement Vault" — Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 (Track 1)

This cheat sheet provides direct, scientifically accurate, and memorable answers tailored for a B.Tech CSE student presenting to hackathon judges.

---

### Q1: What makes "Quantum Heist" different from standard textbook tutorials?
**Answer:**
> *"Many quantum demonstrations are passive scripts or textbook tutorials that simply print a statevector. We transformed foundational quantum principles into a **strategic, mission-based game** with:*  
> 1. *Strict move limits (forcing algorithmic efficiency).*  
> 2. *Interactive security alarms (penalizing quantum self-interference mistakes like $H \cdot H = I$).*  
> 3. *Real-time Qiskit simulation feedback where measurement collapse directly dictates whether vault doors open or security alarms trigger.*  
> *This makes quantum concepts engaging and intuitive for beginners while retaining full mathematical rigor."*

---

### Q2: Why is Qiskit essential? Could this have been done with classical random numbers?
**Answer:**
> *"No. Classical pseudo-random numbers only simulate independent classical probabilities ($P=0.5$). They cannot model quantum phase, unitary transformations, constructive/destructive interference, or tensor-product entanglement.*  
> *In our game, outcomes are generated by compiling genuine quantum gates ($X, Z, H, CNOT$) into a Qiskit `QuantumCircuit` and simulating state evolution with IBM's Qiskit Aer simulator.*  
> *For instance, when an infiltrator applies two Hadamard gates, the wave function destructively interferes for $|1\rangle$ and constructively for $|0\rangle$, producing a 100% deterministic $|0\rangle$ outcome. A classical random number generator cannot reproduce quantum phase interference without hardcoded classical cheats."*

### Q3: How does quantum entanglement work in Door 3 (Master Vault)?
**Answer:**
> *"We start with two qubits in ground state $|00\rangle$.*  
> 1. *Applying a Hadamard gate on Qubit 0 creates an unentangled superposition:*  
>    $$\frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}} \otimes |0\rangle = \frac{|00\rangle + |10\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$$  
> 2. *Applying a Controlled-NOT ($CNOT$) with control $q_0$ and target $q_1$ flips $q_1$ if and only if $q_0$ is 1:*  
>    $$|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{|00\rangle + |11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$$  
> 3. *This is a maximally entangled Bell State. The joint wave function cannot be factored into two independent single-qubit states ($|\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$).*  
> *When measured across 200 shots, the outcomes collapse into either `00` or `11` with ~50% probability each, but mismatched states (`01` and `10`) have an exact probability of **0.0%**. In our game, this guarantees the two separated locks open in perfect synchrony."*

### Q4: Why did you use a simulator (Qiskit Aer) instead of physical IBM Quantum hardware?
**Answer:**
> *"We chose Qiskit Aer for three reasons:*  
> 1. *Interactive Latency: Real quantum hardware jobs wait in IBM Quantum cloud queues (minutes to hours). A playable interactive game requires instant response per turn.*  
> 2. *Educational Baseline: AerSimulator provides a clean reference environment for students to learn ideal gate mechanics without thermal decoherence masking the core physics.*  
> 3. *Hardware Ready: Because our game builds genuine Qiskit `QuantumCircuit` objects, running on real hardware requires only switching the backend to `QiskitRuntimeService().least_busy()`."*

---

### Q5: What are the physical limitations on real NISQ hardware?
**Answer:**
> *"On physical NISQ quantum hardware, two key noise mechanisms would affect our game:*  
> 1. *Decoherence ($T_1$ relaxation and $T_2$ dephasing): Superposition and entanglement degrade over time due to thermal coupling with the environment.*  
> 2. *Gate Infidelity & Readout Errors: Two-qubit gates like $CNOT$ have higher error rates (~0.5%–1.5%) than single-qubit gates (~0.05%), which would cause a small percentage (~1–3%) of spurious mismatch states ('01' or '10') even in a Bell pair.*  
> *In production quantum software, error mitigation techniques like M3 (Matrix Measurement Mitigation) or dynamical decoupling would be required."*

### Q6: Does this project demonstrate "Quantum Advantage"?
**Answer:**
> *"No, and we are completely transparent about that. Simulating 2 qubits requires computing a $4 \times 4$ unitary matrix, which any classical computer can do in microseconds.*  
> *True quantum advantage requires 50+ to 100+ qubits where classical state vector simulation becomes intractable ($2^{100}$). Our project targets **Track 1: Quantum Games & Interactive Puzzles**, focusing on pedagogical gamification and building quantum intuition."*

### Q8: How does the game handle wrong attempts and sequential progression?
**Answer:**
> *"We implemented genuine sequential gating: Door 2 cannot be accessed until Door 1 is cleared, and Door 3 is locked until both Door 1 and 2 are cleared. If a player attempts an invalid circuit or exceeds move limits:*
> 1. *A fair, documented penalty (-25 PTS, floored at 0) is applied, and the mistake counter increments.*
> 2. *The system dynamically regenerates a fresh, solvable puzzle variant for that level (e.g. inverted initial polarity $|1\rangle \to |0\rangle$ on Door 1, shifted phase superposition on Door 2, or anti-correlated $|\Psi^+\rangle$ on Door 3).*
> 3. *This prevents players from simply memorizing a single static answer while keeping the underlying quantum concept consistent."*

### Q9: How does the scoring, timer, and leaderboard persistence work?
**Answer:**
> *"The mission timer starts strictly when the player clicks 'Initiate Quantum Heist' on the start screen, ensuring fair timing.*
> *Points are awarded for completing levels plus bonuses for move efficiency, minus penalties for mistakes.*
> *Upon clearing Door 3, the final score, elapsed time, mistakes, and rank are saved locally to `leaderboard.json`.*
> *We explicitly disclose that the leaderboard is persistent locally to this installation rather than a global cloud backend, avoiding misleading claims while allowing replay and multi-player benchmarking on the demo machine."*
